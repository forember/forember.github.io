## Introduction & Background

Language can be a key barrier to accessing quality healthcare, and so some healthcare providers ensure that they have people on staff who speak Spanish, either to directly provide care, or to translate between a provider and the patient. Language is especially important for effectively navigating the nuances of HIV prevention and treatment, often requiring extended counseling with a provider to land on a prevention or treatment plan. Areas of Mecklenburg County, North Carolina have very high rates of HIV infection. Roughly 3% of residents of ZIP code 28206—encompassing the Double Oaks, Tryon Hills, and Sugar Creek neighborhoods—are living with HIV. Mecklenburg also has over 100,000 adults who speak Spanish, 59% of whom do not speak English very well or at all. Many of these individuals also do not rely on a personal vehicle for getting to work or other daily activities.
